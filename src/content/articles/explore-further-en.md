The atlas is a starting point. These projects offer deeper records, specialist collections, and different ways to travel through cultural heritage.

## Global collections

- [UNESCO World Heritage Centre](https://whc.unesco.org/) — official World Heritage List records, documents, and conservation reporting.
- [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/) — stories, exhibitions, and digitised collections from cultural institutions.
- [Europeana](https://www.europeana.eu/) — art, books, films, music, and archival material from European collections.

## Open knowledge and media

- [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/) — open, structured data connecting places, people, periods, and collections.
- [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/) — freely licensed photographs, drawings, maps, and other media.
- [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/) — a collaboratively maintained map of the world.

## Research habit

Open several sources for the same place. Comparing institutional records, local scholarship, maps, and community-maintained data often reveals the most interesting gaps and disagreements.
